Recommendation

My pickRoute A, "The Deck", built so Route B, "The Forge", bolts on later. Paint a library of a few thousand portraits overnight, sorted by inheritable looks, and ship it with the game. Then, as an upgrade, let the PC mint truly unique children from their parents' faces when the family plays at home. Every piece of A is reused by B, so nothing is wasted.

Why not B first: it needs your PC switched on while anyone plays, and it needs a genetics system in the game before a single face can be requested. A ships a polished game on its own, works offline on the phone, and is the fallback B needs anyway.

The problem in plain words

"Thousands of characters that look hand-painted by one artist, where kids resemble their parents and everyone ages" breaks into four separate jobs. Each has a known tool.

1 · One handEvery face in the same brushwork. Tool: a style LoRA (a small add-on that teaches the model your look), same as saga_ink.
2 · Same frameHead and shoulders, same crop, same eye line, every time. Tool: ControlNet with a few pose templates.
3 · Looks like familyA child's face built from the parents' looks. Tool: the game's genes become prompt words (Route A) or reference images (Route B).
4 · Grows oldThe same person at 8, 25, 50, 70. Tool: an edit model (Klein) that repaints one portrait at a new age.

There is also a fifth, quieter job: where the pictures live. The game is a static site on a phone. It cannot run an AI model, so either the pictures are made ahead of time (A), or a computer at home makes them on request (B).

The two routes

Backup lane: SDXL, if Z-Image disappoints

SDXL with the DMD2 4-step add-on paints a portrait in under 2 seconds and has the biggest library of control tools. Its weakness is obeying detail ("grey eyes, red beard, age 60" often gets ignored), which matters a lot for genes. Z-Image follows instructions far better, which is why it leads. SDXL stays installed as plan C.

The shared foundation (both routes)

1 · A portrait style LoRA

Train on Z-Image (the un-sped-up "base" version, which trains cleaner) with ai-toolkit, 8-bit, at 768 px. Dataset: 40 to 80 portraits we generate and you pick, the same pick-page process as saga_ink. About an evening of training. Taste call for you below: the brand's illustration layer is pen-and-ink; you said painterly. Those are different LoRAs, and they can coexist (ink for icons, paint for faces).

2 · A framing lock

Z-Image ControlNet Union (downloaded today) with three or four head-and-shoulders pose templates. Every portrait gets the same crop and eye line, which is what makes a wall of 40 faces read as one artist and lets the UI assume where the face is.

3 · A gene-to-prompt compiler

A small script: genes in, prompt out, always the same words for the same genes. This is the piece the game will need in its own code too, so I'd write it once in plain JS and use it both to bake the deck and inside the game.

4 · An age and kinship editor

FLUX.2 Klein 4B (on disk) for "the same man at 60" and, in Route B, "the child of these two". FLUX 3 Klein (10 days old, 5.6B, leads the blind tests for exactly this) is the stronger candidate but is gated behind a free Hugging Face licence click. Worth grabbing as an experiment.

5 · Quality gate and finish

The face detector already on disk rejects any portrait with no face, two faces, or a face off-centre, automatically. Then crop to square, grade into the Forge palette (oat skin tones, iron shadows), save WebP. No human looks at 2,400 images; you look at a contact sheet of rejects and a sample.

6 · Your family's faces

No per-person training needed. Klein takes three to five photos of a person as references plus the style LoRA and paints them in the house style. The carly LoRA you already trained is a working fallback if references alone don't hold the likeness.

Risks, honestly

RAM16 GB with swap already in use. Training will run but may crawl or stall at the loading step. A 32 GB kit for this board is about $60 and would remove the risk entirely. Not required to start.

Untested on this card: Z-Image LoRA training on 12 GB is documented by others, not yet by us. The first test run (step 2 below) answers that in one evening.

Painterly edits: Klein is proven for photos; whether it re-ages a painted face without drifting out of style is the real unknown. Step 4 below tests it on five faces before anything depends on it.

Shared faces: in Route A, a world of thousands will reuse identities. Fine for extras, noticeable among jarls you talk to every winter. Jarls and family get the deeper buckets.

The build order

Each step is one session and ends with something you look at. Nothing in the game changes until step 6.

1 · Finish setup. Downloads, trainer install, a one-portrait smoke test of Z-Image + ControlNet. in progress
2 · Style audition. Three painterly looks × 12 portraits on a pick page, same process as saga_ink. You pick; the picks become the training set.
3 · Train portrait LoRA v1 overnight. Contact sheet in the morning.
4 · The two tests that decide everything. A gene grid (does the model obey eye, hair, age, face shape?) and a Klein age/kinship test on five faces.
5 · Bake the Deck. Overnight run, automatic quality gate, you see rejects and a sample.
6 · Genes into the game. The 8-gene schema, inheritance with mutation, age bands, and portraits on every character card.
7 · The Forge (Route B) as the home-wifi upgrade.

Appendix

Numbers behind the estimates

Z-Image Turbo, 8 steps, 1024 px on an RTX 3060: community reports 6 to 9 s per image; I used 8. Klein 4B, 4 steps: 5 to 8 s; the editor pass is counted at 10. Deck of 2,400 at 8 s ≈ 5.3 h, plus 1,800 age edits at 10 s ≈ 5 h: two nights, or one with the quality gate pruning first. Previous SDXL LoRA on this card ran at 1.84 s per step; Z-Image at 768 px in 8-bit is reported at 3 to 5 s per step, so 2,000 steps ≈ 2 to 3 h. WebP at 384 px, painterly, averages 20 to 30 KB.
