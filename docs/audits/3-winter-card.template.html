The winter card

One card turns every winter, about 270 times in a dynasty. It is where sons are born, old men die, kings' fleets come up the fjord and, sometimes, the King dies in his own door. Below is what it does to the player now, read against the dopamine mechanisms, then three live alternatives to tap through. Each is better than the original; they differ in what they make the hero.

What turns, and how often

Cards drawn across eight whole dynasties of your own line (the levi persona from the playtests, 2,175 winters). Births and fleets are two cards in five. The Wanderer turns about 32 times a dynasty, every time with the same sentence. A king's or a stranger's fleet comes about 48 times; the hall falls in about 6 of them.

What it is now

The card never turns. The Autumn sheet's button promises "Into winter · one card turns", and the intro says it too. What arrives is the same grey sheet as every other notice: a title, a sentence, and On. There is no face-down moment and no turn, so nothing builds before the result. anticipation · prediction error
Every card weighs the same. "A son is born" and "Ragnar died in the door of his hall." use the same face, size, color and button. In 808 that second line ends the dynasty. Ragnar is 35, has no son of age and no kin. The next tap reads "The heir", and the one after that reads "The line is broken". proportion
The worst news sits in line order. In 852 the card has six paragraphs. The hall fell; Osric died in the door; "Gudrød is hostile. This is what that costs."; then three old men died in their beds, one after another. Two of them are called Odo, and two Osrics sit on the benches. Lines read "His 0 men walk home unled" and "His 1 men". pruning · proportion
The fight is a code. "(0 vs 101)" and "(200 vs 45)" are two numbers in brackets with no labels. Nothing says the first is the guard you left at home, which is the one part you control. "This is what that costs." also prints on winters when the hall held and nothing was lost. competence · agency
Effects come without numbers. "Upkeep is doubled next winter." "Holdings pay double next summer." "Rumors are half price in spring." The numbers already exist: 98 men at home means the bill goes from 98 to 196. The Wanderer's sentence turns, word for word, about 32 times a dynasty. visible progress · earned, not given
The warnings never come. A hostile king's fleet takes a year to gather (TODO 27). In the winter it starts, the card says nothing, so the fleet's first sign is the fleet. The benches fill at eight and new men stop coming (TODO 26), and nothing says so. the open loop · agency
The King's roll happens offstage. Past fifty, after the card closes, the game rolls the King's death: 10% at 52, 25% at 55. If he lives, the next screen is spring and nothing is said. The band's "death 10%" is the only trace. The game's most suspenseful moment happens where no one can see it. anticipation · peak and end
The death card doesn't say how he died. "Ragnar is dead. 35 winters." reads the same whether he fell in his door or died in his bed. It also says "At most 0 holdings answered to him." and offers "The heir" to a line that has none (TODO 29). peak and end

Verdict

The winter card is a notice where the game promised a draw. Its outcomes are some of the best and worst in the game, from a son for the line to a fleet that kills the King, and it delivers them all in one voice behind one button. The suspense is real; the game has just put it out of sight. Its warnings never reach the player, and its consequences are told as words where numbers exist.

Top three fixes

1. Turn the card, and let its kind set its weight

The card arrives face down for about 350 ms, showing the year, then turns over 520 ms. A tap skips it, and reduced motion shows the face at once. Four tones set the edge, glyph and title color: grave in oxblood (the hall fell, a death), good in pine (a birth, a man from the sea, a harvest, a held hall), warning in chestnut (a hard winter, a hostile king, a feud), and routine in iron. When the King is slain, his death becomes the title: "Ragnar is slain".

2. Say what it cost and what is coming

The fight becomes a labeled bar, Guard 200 against Fleet 45, with "the hall held" or "the hall fell" between. Every effect gets its number: "Next autumn's bill 98 196", "Rumor in spring 20 10 silver", "−18 silver · −11 men". Other news becomes short weighted rows under "also this winter". Old men who died in bed are grouped into one row with the men left unled. Two new rows close TODOs 26 and 27: "Gudrød's fleet is gathering. It sails on the hall next winter if he is still hostile.", and "The benches are full. No new man comes until one is given a holding."

3. Bring the King's roll onstage, and end on what follows

Past fifty, the card carries the King's own winter: "Ragnar, 52 · his winter · 10%". It resolves on screen as "He sees the spring" in pine or "He dies in his sleep" in oxblood. The button at the foot names what comes next: "Spring · 826", or "Ragnar is dead" in oxblood. The death card then names the cause ("Slain in the door of his hall.", or "Died in his bed, the winter Solveig was born.") and shows the heir, or "No son of age. No kin to take the hall. The line is broken."

Missing moments

  • The King's winter. Every winter past fifty is a roll against the dynasty. It is the strongest suspense the game has, and today it is never shown.
  • The warning. A year passes between a king turning hostile and his fleet arriving. That year is a chance to act (gift silver, leave a guard), and the player is never told it has started.
  • The full hall. The day the eighth man sits down without land is the day the hall stops growing. Nothing marks it.
  • The line's count. Births are a fifth of all cards, and none says what the birth does for the line.
  • The cause of death. A king who fell in his door and one who died in his bed get the same card.

Needs a human

  • The turn's timing. A 350 ms back plus a 520 ms turn, 270 times a dynasty, must feel like a breath, not a wait. A tap skips it.
  • Option A asks you to turn the King's card yourself. Decide whether that tap is suspense or a chore by the fifth winter past fifty.
  • Whether the King's roll should move onto the card at all, or stay a surprise you only see when it kills him. The mocks show it; the odds don't change.
  • Whether grouping old men's deaths into one row hides someone you cared about. A named man with a holding may deserve his own row.
  • The duplicate names (two Odos, two Osrics). That's a naming fix in the game, not on this card; it is logged either way.

Three alternatives

Each phone is live, and every state is a real save from the playtests. Press the button at the foot to see where the card hands off: in 846 and 808 it goes to the new death card. In 820, choose. Replay runs the opening again. All three share fixes 2 and 3 (the numbers, the warnings, the roll, the death card); they differ in what carries the moment.

Option A

The Turn

my pick

A real card, turned.

The winter night fills the screen and one card sits in the middle, face down, showing the year. It turns. Its edge, glyph and title take the tone of what it says. Under it, the other news of the winter arrives as short rows. Past fifty, a second, smaller card lies face down with the King's odds on its back, and the button at the foot reads "Turn his card". You turn it.

  • The copy has promised "one card turns" since session 2. This keeps the promise.
  • The reliable baseline (a routine card turns) makes the rare grave card land.
  • The King's card is the only tap that adds suspense rather than reading.

Risk: the most motion of the three. The turn must stay under a second and skip on tap.

Option B

The Hall at Night

The hall is the picture; the event happens to it.

Above the text, a small drawing of the hall: the fjord running to the horizon, the King's high seat, eight bench seats, the children in a row. Hostile kings' sails stand on the horizon, solid when their fleet is out, dashed while it gathers. The card acts on the drawing: a fleet runs down the fjord and breaks or burns the hall, a seat empties in oxblood, a child appears in pine.

  • The full bench and the gathering fleet are always visible, not only on the winter they change.
  • The King's roll resolves on its own after a beat.

Risk: the most to build, and it overlaps screen 5, the Hall pane. The drawing is small on a phone.

Option C

The Saga Page

Each winter is a line in the reign's saga.

The winter is written as one saga sentence, word by word, in the display face: "Gudrød's fleet came up the fjord and the hall fell. Osric died in its door." Numbers sit under it as footnotes. An Ahead box carries what is coming. At the foot, a strip of every winter of this reign, one tile each and colored by tone, so a reign takes a visible shape over the years.

  • The cheapest to build: text, a strip, and the chronicle the game already keeps.
  • Ahead adds the King's odds for next winter, not only this one.

Risk: still mostly reading. The least surprise; the word-by-word reveal is the only build-up.
