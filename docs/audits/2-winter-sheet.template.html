The Winter sheet

What it is now

The money happened offstage. The band says 261 silver. The sheet says Vestfold paid nothing and 47 men ate 47. Nowhere does the chest go from last spring's number to this one: the loot that came home in summer, the rents that came in, the bill that went out. Verdict

The Winter sheet is a receipt with a shop stapled to it. It is honest and it is complete, and it treats the best and worst things that can happen in a year as the same kind of sentence. The year's income, the one number a raiding king lives for, is never shown arriving. The market asks a question without saying what the answer buys. And the way out is the quiet button.

Top three fixes

1. Account for the year, and let the chest count

Open the sheet on a ledger: the chest in spring, loot in from the summer's raid, rent in from each holding, the hall's bill out, and the chest now. Rows arrive one after another, about 120 ms apart, and the total counts from last spring's number to this one over 650 ms, up in pine or down in oxblood. Routine zeros become quiet rows ("Vestfold · the King was at sea · 0"), so the sentence that repeated 273 times is gone and the number it hid is visible.

2. Weigh the lines by what they cost

A lost holding or a starving hall is set in the display face in oxblood with a stripe; a new holding in pine; a realm's growth as a muted note. Routine lines leave the list entirely. A title's payoff shows where it lands: "47 men ate 47 24 · Deep-chest", "27 men ate 27 54 · hard winter", "3 2 a man · Fair-dealer". Most years the list is short or empty, so the year something happens, it lands.

3. Make the market a decision, and the exit the hero

The stepper stays. Under it, what the men buy: "+40 ship strength under Ragnar" from the real combat number, and a room bar showing how many more the named men can lead. The lesson about price and upkeep shows in 793 and never again. When silver cannot buy five men, the market collapses to one dashed line and the sheet is short. One primary at the bottom, always on screen: "Into winter · one card turns". Buy is a quiet button inside the market card.

Missing moments

  • The haul. Loot is announced on the fight sheet in summer and never seen again. The autumn account is where it should land in the chest.
  • The rent roll. A king with four holdings never sees four rents add up. The ledger gives him a growing column, year on year.
  • The title's first payoff. The winter Deep-chest first halves the bill, or Fair-dealer first drops the price, should read as the reward it is.
  • A quiet year. When nothing happened on the land, the sheet should be short and say so, so that the loud years are loud.
  • The season's name. The sheet is the reckoning before winter; winter is the card that follows. The mock-ups call the sheet Autumn and the exit "Into winter", so the card is something the button promises.

Needs a human

  • The ledger's cadence. Rows at 120 ms and a 650 ms count is a breath; 270 times over it must not become a wait. Reduced motion skips it.
  • Option C changes a rule: bought men go straight to the first named man with room. Today they sit unled until Fill. Decide whether the rule or the picture should change.
  • Whether "Autumn" is the right word on a Viking sheet, or whether the sheet should keep "Winter" and the card get a name of its own.
  • The stripe and oxblood on a lost holding: strong enough to land, quiet enough not to feel like an error dialog. Your phone is the judge.

Three alternatives

Each phone is live. Step the count and Buy to see the numbers move. Press the exit to see where the sheet hands off. The year buttons load real saved states from a driven dynasty: 793 is the first winter, 806 a good haul in a hard winter, 835 a busy year on the land, 850 a chest too poor to buy, 802 a hall that could not feed everyone. Option A

The Ledger

my pick

The year as an account.

The sheet opens on the chest in spring, then loot, rents and the bill arrive as rows, and the chest counts to its new number. Beneath it, only the events that mattered, weighted by kind. The market is one card with the stepper, what the men buy next summer, and the room among the named men. One primary at the foot: Into winter.

  • Routine zeros are rows, not sentences. Titles show where they pay.
  • Lost holding or starvation: oxblood, display face, a stripe. New holding: pine.
  • Nothing to buy: the market is one dashed line and the sheet is short.
  • The first winter teaches price, upkeep and leading once, under the market.

Risk: the most text of the three. The ledger must stay short, so it never shows more than one row per holding.

Option B

The Chest

One number, and the bar that made it.

The chest is the hero: a large count from last spring's silver to now, and under it a bar that draws the year, grey for what was kept, teal for loot, pine for rent, oxblood struck through the right end for what the hall ate. Buying men grows a chestnut segment into the bar live, so the price is felt as a bite out of the year. Events sit below as weighted lines; the exit is the primary.

  • The bar is to scale, so a good year looks long and a poor one short.
  • The stepper and the Buy are the same as A; the bar is the sum.
  • Quiet years are a single italic line under the chest.

Risk: the bar hides the rent roll by holding; four rents are one pine segment. The year reads at a glance but not per coast.

Option C

The Muster

The men are the hero; the money is four tiles.

The account is four small tiles across the top: loot, rent, ate, chest. Then the named men, each with a bar of his following against what he can lead, the King first. Stepping the count fills the first bar with room in pine, so you see exactly who the new men will follow; Buy settles the pine into teal. Events as weighted lines; Into winter at the foot.

  • Bought men go to the first man with room, King first, as Fill does today. That is a rule change.
  • Men away at a holding carry a teal tag with the coast's name.
  • Past five men, the rest fold into one line with their shared room.

Risk: the boldest. Winter becomes the muster screen, which overlaps what the Hall pane does in spring. The money is small.
