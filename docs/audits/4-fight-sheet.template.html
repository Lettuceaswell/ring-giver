The Fight sheet

After Sail, the summer is a short run of sheets: Landfall when the coast is new, the fight, Plunder or stay when the coast can be held, and a card for any named man who fell. The fight comes in almost every summer, about 270 times a dynasty, and it's the only roll of the dice the player makes. Below is what these sheets do to the player now, then three live alternatives to tap through.

What a summer brings, and how often

These are the fights of eight whole dynasties of your own line (the levi persona from the playtests): 2,168 fights in about 2,180 summers. More than half are routs. One in six is lost. Named men fall in one fight in seven, and the King himself falls about seven or eight times a dynasty. Landfall and Plunder or stay each come in about one summer in five. The roll changes the winner in only 6% of fights; the rest are sure or hopeless before the ship sails. But it sets the loot and the losses in every one.

What it is now

The verdict arrives before the fight. The bar grows for 0.6 s, but "Rout." is already printed under it in the same frame. The day's roll swings the ship's strength by up to 15% either way. It sets the loot and the losses of every fight, decides between a rout and a narrow thing, and now and then decides who wins. It's never shown. Nothing builds before the result, so the summer ends in a word you read, not a moment you watch. anticipation · prediction error
The odds are rewritten after the roll. The bar and its words use the strength after the roll, so a lost fight always looks worse than the odds you sailed on and a won one looks better. In 838 the ship sailed on 81% ("likely"), had a bad day, and lost; the card calls it "even". In 967 Bjorn sailed on 76% and won, and the card calls it "sure". The odds the commit sheet showed are on the scene object and never drawn. Nobody can tell bad luck from a bad plan. competence · learning
A raid's real price is hidden. "28 silver in loot. 84 men lost." (921). At 3 silver a man, that raid spent about 250 silver of men to bring home 28. The card doesn't add it up. It doesn't say Lindisfarne now stands at 1,000 defenders, up from 867, or whose coast it was and how far he soured. The men are a number. Losses are shared across the jarls, but no one is named. What the men earned is silent: Follow +1 on a rout ("The men will follow these jarls anywhere." is the only trace), Combat +1 every third raid, stance up for a share of the loot. Verdict

The fight is the one roll of the dice in a summer, and the sheet reports it instead of rolling it. The result comes before the fight, the odds are judged with hindsight, the men are a count, and the sums of a raid or a holding are left to the player. The summer's best decision, plunder or stay, comes as a page of rules.

Top three fixes

1. Roll the dice on screen

An odds strip under the coast's name. It spans the roll's whole range, from a bad day (0.85 of the ship's strength) to a good one (1.15), banded by what each day would bring: driven back, a narrow thing, victory, rout. Everything right of the oxblood is a win, so its share is exactly the chance you sailed on: 76% at Galicia. The loot each band would bring sits under it. A needle swings and lands on the day, and only then does the verdict stamp in. About 1.2 s, and a tap skips it. The math is unchanged: the bands come from the stored odds and the verdict's own thresholds, and the day is rolled strength ÷ strength.

2. Count the raid and name the men

The raid's account: loot into the chest, men lost and what they were worth, the net, the coast arming ("Lindisfarne 867 → 1,000 defenders"), and a ruler's stance when it was his coast. Then the crew, one row a named man: men lost, Follow or Combat up, the stance arrow. The fallen are on the fight card in oxblood, with the men they leave unled. When the King falls, his fall is the title ("Bjorn falls at Galicia"). The foot button says what follows: "Plunder or stay", "Home · Autumn 921", "Bjorn is dead".

3. Make plunder or stay a trade, and give Landfall a way home

Two tiles: Plunder · 125 now against Stay · 31 a year, five summers to earn it back. Then one line of rules and the ruler's price ("Anund's seat · he sours, cool → sour"). Each jarl shows his chance to hold next summer, both with the men he has and with his full following: "holds if you send 12 men in spring". At Landfall, when nothing is better than long odds, a dashed Turn for home sits under the coasts: the summer is spent, no blood.

Missing moments

  • The day. Luck decides about one fight in four, and the player never sees it land.
  • The jarl's rise. The third raid makes a man a better fighter, and a rout makes his men follow him further. Both happen in silence.
  • The bad bargain. Late in the game a coast stands at 1,000 defenders and a win costs more men than it brings in silver. A winning sheet hides a losing trade.
  • The first holding. Leaving the first jarl on a foreign shore is the start of a realm. It reads the same as the fortieth.
  • The way home. A blind landfall with nothing worth fighting has no exit.

Needs a human

  • The needle's timing. 1.2 s, 270 times a dynasty, has to feel like a breath, not a wait. A tap skips it. In most fights the needle only picks between a rout and a victory, and the loot that goes with each. Judge whether that is stake enough.
  • Turn for home is a new verb, and it makes sailing blind cheaper: you lose the summer, not the men. It matches the GDD ("a season gone"), but it's a rule change. Your call.
  • Men valued at the market price (3 silver) in the raid's net. It's honest, but men also cost a silver a winter to keep, so a raid that "loses" silver may still be worth it to a hall with too many mouths. The net may push the player off raiding more than you want.
  • Late-game crews of eight or nine named men make a long roll call. The mocks fold men who led no one into one line.

Found on the way (not fixed)

  • Board all puts named men with no men aboard. In 838 eight named men sailed, seven of them leading no one. They add nothing to the ship, and each can fall: Styr and Hastein did. Board all should skip a man with no men to lead, or the commit sheet should say what it risks.
  • Late raids lose money. In your line past 900, 88% of fights bring home less silver than the men they cost (13% before 900). Lindisfarne, Galicia and Tours sit at 867 to 1,000 defenders, and wins there bring 25 to 46 silver for 58 to 84 men. The coasts arm as designed; the screen just never says it. Also: most lost fights in your line, at least 242 of 374, were hopeless before the ship sailed.
  • Landfall's little map piles labels up on the Channel coast (912), the same pile-up as round 1. Pinch-zoom (TODO 15) is the fix.

Three alternatives

Each phone is live, and every state is a real save from the playtests. Most are from your own line, and 838 is the raider's. Tap the coasts at Landfall, choose at Plunder or stay, and press the foot button to see where each sheet hands off. Replay runs the fight again, and a tap on the fight skips its beat. All three share fixes 2 and 3 and the odds strip. They differ in what carries the moment.

Option A

The Clash

my pick

The fight is a moment you watch.

Two ranks face each other across a line: the ship's strength as marks, grouped by the man who leads them, the King's in the accent, and the coast's defenders opposite. The odds strip sits under them. The needle swings and lands, the ranks close, the lost marks go oxblood, and the verdict stamps in. The account and the crew follow as rows.

  • The roll becomes the event, and it happens every summer.
  • Most days are routs, so a fight that goes the other way lands hard.
  • The marks show size at a glance: a ship of 1,400 against 400 looks like it.

Risk: the most motion of the three, every summer. It must stay near a second and skip on tap.

Option B

The Raid on the Chart

The fight happens where it happens.

The sheet stays low and the map stays lit, zoomed to the route from Vestfold to the coast. The defenders are a ring around the coast. The needle lands, the ring breaks in pine or holds in oxblood, and the loot runs home along the route into the band, where the chest counts up. Plunder or stay draws the coast as a holding, with its rent and its unrest ring. At Landfall each coast's chance is printed on the map, and you can tap the coast itself.

  • Carries round 1's Chart through the summer: the map is the game.
  • The loot landing in the chest is the most direct reward of the three.

Risk: the crew rows get less room. Far coasts (Galicia, Munster) zoom the map out until the coast is small.

Option C

The Roll Call

The men are the story.

The crew is the body of the sheet: one row a named man with a bar of his men. After the needle the rows resolve one by one: his losses eat into his bar in oxblood, then his gains and stance arrow appear. A man who fell turns oxblood and stays in the list. The King leads it. The account is a footer. Plunder or stay is the same roll call: each jarl's row is the choice, with his chance to hold.

  • Makes the dynasty's real asset, its named men, the thing each summer changes.
  • The cheapest to build: rows the Hall pane already knows how to draw.

Risk: still a list to read. Late crews are long, and the verdict is smaller.

Undecided. Pick one, or take parts across them. Whatever you pick, fixes 2 and 3 go into the game with it. Turn for home waits on your yes.
