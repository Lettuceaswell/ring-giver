What it is now

The map is a picture of Europe, not of your world. In 793 the player sees Italy and the Black Sea under five dots in Scandinavia. The SVG is taller than its viewBox, so land outside the crop leaks into the letterbox. Fog, the thing the design doc calls the map's best trick, is invisible: unknown coastline is drawn as brightly as known coastline. visible progress · pruning
The band wraps badly. Tags and "fork at 45" break across lines and leave a stray dot at a line start. "10 unled / 48 men" is a ratio in jargon on the most-looked-at pixels. The death percentage, the one line that builds dread, is the smallest thing in the band. agency · anticipation

Verdict

The spring screen is honest and flat. It shows the right facts in the same voice every year, so nothing on it ever beats expectation. It has no arrival, no departure, and no sense of distance travelled across the centuries. The one surface that could carry all three, the map, is drawn as a static chart with dots on it. The commit, Sail, is a plain button far from the odds it commits to. The player reads, taps, and leaves; the screen never says "you have come far" or "this one matters."

Top three fixes

1. Make the fog real, and let the crop follow it

Light the known world: a soft pool of brighter sea and coastline around every known coast, dimmer land and deeper sea everywhere else. Crop to the lit pool with the aspect ratio matched to the container so nothing leaks. In 793 the lit world is one fjord; by 900 it is the whole North Sea. Progress across a dynasty becomes something you see, not count.

visible progressgoal gradientpruning visual · one SVG mask with a blur, no new state

2. Put the odds inside the commit

One object does the committing: the target's name, the odds in words, and the verb, with the odds' temperature on the button itself (pine for sure, teal for likely, chestnut for even, oxblood for long odds). What you are doing, how likely, and go, in one glance and one tap. The separate odds card and destination card go.

congruenceagencyproportion visual · color · information

3. Give spring an arrival beat

When the year turns and the Map pane comes into view, about 800ms of motion with nothing to dismiss: silver and men count from last year's number to this one, coasts first seen last summer bloom out of the fog, the route to the standing destination draws itself from Vestfold. Then the screen is still. The same beat, smaller, when a coast is tapped: the route redraws, the odds bar slides.

Missing moments

  • Arrival. No ceremony marks a new year on the screen where the year is lived.
  • Departure. Sail is a cut. The fleet never visibly leaves.
  • Fog lifting at home. Coasts are revealed in Landfall and simply exist afterwards. The home map never shows the world growing.
  • The centuries. Nothing at 900 says the world has opened since 793. One line, "18 of 48 coasts known", or the lit pool itself, would.
  • Distance to the end. "fork at 45" and "death 15%" are facts. Neither reads as a thing approaching.

Needs a human

  • The length of the arrival beat. The mock-ups use 800ms. It has to feel like a breath, not a wait, 270 times over.
  • Whether the route should animate every spring or only draw once and then sit still until the target changes.
  • Option C's prose: does reading one sentence a year become a chore by turn 100?
  • Label size and the fog blur on your actual phone. The blur is a filter on an SVG mask; it should be fine, but older iPhones are the test.

Three alternatives

Each phone is live. Tap any coast to change the destination. Press Sail to see the departure. Use the buttons above each phone to replay the arrival beat and to switch between 793 (the first spring) and 815. Ship and Hall tabs are not built here.

Option A

The Chart

my pick

The map is the screen.

The map runs edge to edge behind a translucent band and a single commit sheet. The known world is a lit pool; everything else is in fog. Rumor and the crew are two chips over the water. The hero is the lit world itself, which grows for 273 years.

  • Band: year, silver and men; name, age and tags on the next line; a life strand past fifty.
  • Commit sheet: target, loot and defenders, odds bar, one plank with the odds words and temperature.
  • Arrival beat: numbers count, fog expands, new coasts bloom, route draws.
  • First spring: the plank says "Choose a coast", Agder pulses, then the crew chip pulses.

Risk: a full-bleed map leaves less room for text; the crop has to be good every year. Pinch-zoom belongs here when it lands.

Option B

The Plank

The current shape, with one hero.

Band, framed map, one card, Sail bar, tabs, as today. Three changes: the map has fog and a tight crop; the three cards under it become one; and the Sail button is a plank whose fill is the odds bar. The commit and the odds are literally the same object.

  • One card: the target row, a crew strip (who sails, who stays, All aboard), the Wanderer as a single line.
  • The plank: teal to the odds, chestnut past them; the label sits on the bar.
  • Arrival beat: numbers count and last summer's new coasts flash once.

Risk: the safest of the three. It fixes every finding but keeps the map as a picture in a frame.

Option C

The Saga Page

The year as a page of the saga.

The screen reads like a chronicle entry: a big year, three written lines from the state of the world, a framed chart beneath, and the commit as a sentence. The words change every year because the world does, so the screen is never the same twice. Numbers are set in accent inside the prose.

  • Prose from state: the king's age, the chest and the hall, the standing target with its spears and silver, the Wanderer at the door.
  • Tapping a place name in the prose or a coast on the chart sets the target.
  • Arrival beat: the lines come in one after another, then the chart.

Risk: the boldest. It asks the player to read a sentence a year; on turn 200 that may be a chore. The chart is smaller.
